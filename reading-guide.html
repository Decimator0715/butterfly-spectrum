Read the animation

The left panel collects allowed energies as magnetic flux changes. The right panel resolves energies along the momentum path ky = kx at the current flux. Matching colored gap bars refer to the same global energy interval. The red column projects the selected red rank branch, not the density of states. The complete black background is visible from the beginning in the current 29 October videos. A scan in flux is a parameter sweep, not real-time dynamics.

Separate labels from open gaps

With reduced flux α = p∕q and r occupied bands, the integer candidates obey

ν = r∕q = 𝑚𝛼 + g,𝑝𝑚 + 𝑞𝑔 = r.

If (m0,g0) is a solution, so is (m0 + 𝑛𝑞,g0 − 𝑛𝑝) . The integer equation alone does not select the physical Chern branch. It also does not determine whether a gap is open, how wide it is, or its absolute energy.

The present nearest-neighbor square-lattice convention shows an independent target of order |m| only for q > 2|m| and only when its computed global gap is open. At q = 2|m| the central gap closes. Smaller denominators give a lower-order representative or no internal gap. This rule is not a general prescription for triangular lattices or models with additional hopping terms.

Direct separation and a global gap

For ordered bands,

dr = min ⁡ 𝐤(Er+1 − Er),Δr = min ⁡ 𝐤Er+1 − max ⁡ 𝐤Er.

Positive direct separation isolates a rank-r subspace at each momentum. A positive global gap permits one fixed Fermi energy between the two bands at all momenta. These statements must not be conflated. The animation reports the global width; a jump in a selected colored branch is not a gap measurement.

What the branch colors establish

The animation uses zero-coupling rank continuation: it selects the coupled eigenvalue at the energy rank of a chosen uncoupled momentum replica, separately at each momentum. This neither tracks an eigenvector through a coupling sweep nor directly tests its central momentum weight. The colored branch is therefore not a validated wavefunction-based IEB. Replica indices and gap labels are different objects, especially when indices coincide modulo q.

A useful reading route

  • Chapter 6: Harper model, rational flux, spectral sets and finite resolution.
  • Chapters 8–9: Berry phases, isolated subspaces, orientation and Chern numbers.
  • Chapter 10: gap labels, integer candidates and Wannier lines.
  • Chapters 2–4: Farey arithmetic and specific hierarchical constructions.
  • Chapter 12: experimental evidence as reviewed in 2016, not a current survey.

Workflow: specify model and flux units → identify the spectral gap → list integer candidates → select the physical branch with independent evidence → interpret the response under its filling and sign conventions.
