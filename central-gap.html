Why the central gap closes at even denominators

Model and conclusion

Use the nearest-neighbor square-lattice Harper equation, with lattice spacing and transverse hopping normalized to one:

un+1 + un−1 + 2λcos⁡ (2𝜋𝑝𝑛 q + ky) un = Eun.
un+q = e𝑖𝑞kx un,gcd ⁡ (p,q) = 1,q = 2L.

The parameter λ is real; λ = 1 is the isotropic case. Since the fraction is reduced and q is even, p is odd. In the complete two-dimensional magnetic Brillouin zone, bands L and L + 1 touch at zero energy. The central global gap is therefore exactly zero.

Construct two exact zero-energy states

Choose ky = π/2. The on-site potential then satisfies

vn = −2λsin⁡ (2𝜋𝑝𝑛 q ),v0 = vL = 0,vq−n = −vn.

At E = 0, define the transfer matrices

A(v) = ( −v −1 1 0 ),J = A(0).
( un+1 un ) = A(vn) ( un un−1 ) .

Direct multiplication gives

det ⁡ A(v) = 1,J2 = −I,A(−v) = −𝐽𝐴(v)−1J−1.

Set B = A(vL−1)⋯A(v1) , with B = I when L = 1. The transfer matrix over a full magnetic period is

Mq = A(vq−1)⋯A(vL+1)𝐽𝐵𝐽.

Opposite potentials occur in reverse order, so

A(vq−1)⋯A(vL+1) = (−1)L−1JB−1J−1.
Mq = (−1)L−1JB−1J−1𝐽𝐵𝐽 = (−1)LI.

This is exact for every L and real λ; no weak-coupling expansion is used. Choose the Bloch phase to satisfy

e𝑖𝑞kx = (−1)L.

Thus kx = 0 for even L, or kx = π/q for odd L, modulo 2π/q. Every two-component initial condition satisfies the Bloch boundary condition. The nearest-neighbor recurrence is uniquely determined by two initial values, so the finite Bloch Hamiltonian has a two-dimensional zero-energy eigenspace. For q = 2, the two neighboring paths must be added rather than overwritten.

Identify the central bands and the global gap

Define a half-period translation and an alternating sign operation:

(TLu)n = un+L,(𝐷𝑢)n = (−1)nu n,S = DTL.

Even q ensures that both operations preserve the same Bloch boundary condition. Odd p gives vn+L = − vn . Translation changes the sign of the potential, while the alternating sign changes the sign of the hopping. At the same momentum,

𝑆𝐻S−1 = −H.

The energies occur in ±E pairs. At the contact point there are exactly two zero-energy states; the remaining states consist of L − 1 negative and L − 1 positive energies. Consequently the zero energies are precisely EL and EL+1 .

EL(𝐤) ≤ 0 ≤ EL+1(𝐤).

The contact point saturates both bounds:

max ⁡ 𝐤EL = 0 = min ⁡ 𝐤EL+1.
ΔL = min ⁡ 𝐤EL+1 − max ⁡ 𝐤EL = 0.

Energy-reflection symmetry alone does not prove closure. The exact double zero-energy solution is the additional essential step.

Gap-label candidates

For the candidate relation between the band count r and the integer m,

r ≡ 𝑝𝑚(𝑚𝑜𝑑q),pq 2 ≡ q 2(modq).

Thus m = q/2 maps to the central band count r = q/2. This is a correspondence between candidate labels. At the closed central gap, one cannot assign a well-defined Chern number to the occupied subspace separated by that gap.

Two paths in the momentum-space convention

This section uses the project convention, distinct from the real-space normalization above:

Kn = kx + nG2,G1 = 2π,G2 = 2𝜋𝛼.
𝜀n = −2t1 cos⁡(kx + 2𝜋𝛼𝑛),h = −t2.
H0,q−1 = he−𝑖𝜃,𝜃 = qk y.

For reduced p/q there are q independent momentum states. The Bragg partner of 0 is −m. At q = 2m, +m and −m are the same state modulo q, not two independent partners. For q = 2 the two adjacency contributions must be summed.

Take m positive and q = 2m. Since p is odd, choose g = (1−p)/2. The positive Bragg plane is

kx = π(𝑚𝛼 + g) = π 2 , kx G1 = 1 4.
𝜀0 = 𝜀m = 0,𝜀−j = −𝜀j.

The paths 0 → 1 → ··· → m and 0 → −1 → ··· → −m ≡ m both contain m steps. For m less than q/2, the two arcs instead have lengths m and q − m. At the central Bragg plane, neither equal-order channel may be discarded.

Interference: perturbative expression and exact elimination

Where the intermediate denominators are nonzero, the leading amplitudes are

V +(m) = hm ∏ j=1m−1(−𝜀j).
V −(m) = (−1)m−1e−𝑖𝜃V +(m).

Their cancellation condition is e−𝑖𝜃 = (−1)m . The project slice ky = kx satisfies it at kx = π/2.

For the exact argument at m ≥ 2, retain states 0 and m and eliminate the two complementary chains of length m − 1. In the forward and reverse ordering, their Hamiltonians obey

H− = −DH+D,G± = (0 − H±)−1,G − = −DG+D.

Here D is the alternating diagonal matrix on a complementary chain. This formula requires the complementary blocks to be invertible at zero energy. The diagonal self-energies cancel, and the endpoint Green functions obey

Σ0(0) = Σm(0) = 0,(G−)1,m−1 = (−1)m−1(G +)1,m−1.

The exact Schur off-diagonal element is

V eff(0) = Am [1 + (−1)m−1e−𝑖𝜃] ,A m = hm det ⁡ (−H+).

At the cancellation phase, the full two-by-two effective block vanishes and both zero-energy states remain. If the complementary block is singular, the inverse formula cannot be used directly. At fixed nonzero t1 , its determinant is a polynomial in t2 that is nonzero at t2 = 0; singular values are isolated, and the eigenvalue statement extends by continuity. The transfer-matrix proof above does not rely on this inverse.

For m = 1, or q = 2, there is no intermediate chain:

V eff = h(1 + e−𝑖𝜃),𝜃 = π.

The two contributions cancel directly.

What this means for branch selection

A branch-selection rule cannot create or remove an eigenvalue gap. At degeneracy, eigenvectors may rotate within the degenerate subspace, so the central weight of a single numerical eigenvector is not unique. Keep the subspace and its common energy; a tied score or NaN alone does not establish a closed gap.

The cancellation occurs at particular ky values, not on every fixed slice. For example, at α = 1/4 and the central Bragg plane,

V eff(0) = t22 2t1 (e−i4ky − 1).

For t1 = 1, t2 = 0.75 and kx = π/2, the central direct separation at ky = π/4 is approximately 0.9154759474. At ky = π/2 the bands touch exactly. The full two-dimensional spectrum therefore has no central global gap.

Scope, source and downloads

The proof applies to the stated nearest-neighbor rectangular model with real λ. Additional diagonal hopping, on-site terms or orbitals require the symmetry and transfer relations to be checked again. A fixed arbitrary ky slice need not contain the contact point.

The source document records symbolic checks and high-precision numerical examples. They support the algebra but do not replace the general proof, and were not rerun for this webpage conversion. The source cites I. Krasovsky, Central spectral gaps of the almost Mathieu operator, arXiv:1602.08624v2, PDF pages 1–3, for the standard even-denominator result; it does not attribute every step of this locally reconstructed derivation to that paper.
